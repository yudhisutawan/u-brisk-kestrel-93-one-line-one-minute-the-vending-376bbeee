# Tournament — One Line, One Minute: The Vending Machine Pickup Line Contest

Judge brief (from the kit, criteria in priority order):

1. The line is a pickup line a vending machine would use on a passing human,
   spoken by the machine itself.
2. It uses the machine's own world: slot, buttons, coils, snacks, hum,
   exact change.
3. It lands a laugh, with the turn/punch arriving in the last few words.
4. Short and speakable, one breath, nothing tacked on.
5. Charming rather than creepy — the human smiles instead of walking faster.

Gate: entry.txt must be UTF-8, 1–200 characters, not only whitespace.
Tie-breaks favour the smaller text, so short lines also help there.

## Elimination round (criteria-ordered)

- **H2 out** — "my slot hasn't felt this warm" reads suggestive; fails
  criterion 5 (charming vs creepy), and the "morning shift" punch is weak.
- **H5 out** — no clear machine speaker ("You and I" could be anyone);
  weakest on criterion 1, the most important one.
- **H7 out** — "slide it in again" is suggestive; fails criterion 5 despite
  a very relatable setup.
- **L12 out** — the twist ("that's me") lands mid-line, not in the last few
  words (criterion 3), and only one machine element (criterion 2).

Key head-to-heads:

- **H4 vs L2**: both strongly machine-voiced; H4's "stale bag of chips on
  your toe" is a sharper, more visual/absurd punch than "I don't even like
  Snickers". H4 wins narrowly.
- **H4 vs H3**: both dense in machine world; H4's "love at first press"
  wordplay is a truer pickup-line shape with the punch on the last word.
  H4 wins narrowly.
- **H4 vs H1**: H1 opens with a generic bar line; H4 is machine-specific
  from the first word. H4 wins.
- **H3 vs L2**: H3's error-code punch is more surprising and machine-specific
  than L2's self-aware Snickers twist. H3 wins narrowly.

## Challenger check

- **L13 vs H4**: L13 ("my last stale chips") adds machine sacrifice but
  "a stale bag of chips" is more concrete and visual; the punch lands
  harder in H4. H4 wins by a hair. No rebuild needed — H4 is the clear

## Winner

> **H4 (Hermes): "Do you believe in love at first press, or should I drop a stale bag of chips on your toe?" — 89 characters, 7–0 in the round robin.**

Why it wins: unmistakably the machine speaking from word one ("press",
"drop"); dense machine world (press, drop, stale chips); the classic
"love at first sight" pickup shape twisted into machine terms; the punch
("on your toe") arrives on the very last word; short, speakable, and
playfully threatening rather than creepy — a joke, not a come-on.
